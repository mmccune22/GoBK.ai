## The protection does not expire

A bankruptcy discharge is a permanent court order prohibiting collection of covered debts as your personal liability.

A creditor cannot undo that protection by selling the account, assigning it a new number, or hiring a different collector. A debt buyer acquires the account subject to the discharge.

Voluntarily paying part of a discharged debt does not, by itself, restore the creditor’s right to collect the rest. The Code permits [voluntary repayment](/library/loans-from-family-and-friends), but that is different from an enforceable obligation.

A promise to repay a discharged debt generally cannot bypass the formal requirements governing reaffirmation. Be cautious about signing a new agreement based on an old discharged balance.

## Why an old obligation may still be enforceable

### It was not discharged

Some debts survive because the law excludes them from discharge. Common examples include qualifying [support obligations](/library/child-support-alimony-and-bankruptcy), [certain taxes](/library/can-old-tax-debt-be-discharged), and most [student loans](/library/student-loans-and-bankruptcy) unless the required additional relief was obtained.

Other debts may be covered by a court ruling that they are nondischargeable. Debts arising after filing also generally require separate analysis.

An [omitted creditor](/library/which-debts-does-bankruptcy-erase) raises additional questions. Whether an unlisted debt was discharged depends on the chapter, notice, the nature of the debt, and other circumstances. Listing a debt does not guarantee discharge, and omitting one does not produce the same result in every case.

### You entered an enforceable reaffirmation agreement

A valid [reaffirmation agreement](/library/reaffirmation-retain-redeem) preserves personal liability for a debt that otherwise could have been discharged.

Signing a document alone does not necessarily make it enforceable. Reaffirmation has requirements involving timing, disclosures, filing, and, in some cases, court approval.

If the agreement is enforceable and you later default, the creditor may retain personal collection rights. The debt has not “come back”; the agreement prevented that liability from being eliminated.

### A lien survived

Discharging personal liability generally does not eliminate a valid lien that was not avoided or otherwise resolved.

A mortgage lender may still foreclose against its collateral, or a vehicle lender may retain repossession rights, even though it cannot collect a discharged deficiency from you personally.

Judgment liens can also survive unless properly addressed. Receiving a discharge does not automatically clear the title to every asset.

### The court revoked the discharge

Revocation is an exceptional court remedy available on specific statutory grounds.

In Chapter 7, those grounds include certain fraud in obtaining the discharge, knowing and fraudulent failure to report or surrender estate property, and specified failures involving court orders or audits.

The deadlines vary. Some requests must be brought within one year after discharge; others may be brought by the later of that date or the case’s closing date. There is no single one-year rule for every Chapter 7 revocation request.

Chapter 13 has a separate fraud-based revocation provision and deadline. A creditor cannot revoke a discharge simply by declaring it invalid or restarting collection.

## Does reopening the case undo the discharge?

No. Reopening a bankruptcy case does not, by itself, revoke the discharge.

A case may be reopened to address estate property, resolve a dispute, or enforce the discharge against a creditor. Receiving notice that a case has reopened is therefore not the same as losing its protection.

## What to do if collection resumes

Start with records rather than assuming the demand is valid.

1. **Identify the debt.** Compare the original creditor, account information, and dates with your bankruptcy records.
2. **Preserve the communication.** Keep letters, statements, emails, voicemails, and records of payments or attempted withdrawals.
3. **Locate your case documents.** Save the discharge order, creditor schedules, and any relevant reaffirmation, lien, or dischargeability orders.
4. **Have the demand evaluated.** Determine whether it seeks discharged personal liability, enforcement of a surviving lien, or payment of an obligation outside the discharge.

Do not ignore a lawsuit or court deadline because you believe the debt was discharged. The discharge may provide a defense or grounds for bankruptcy-court relief, but the papers still require prompt attention.

See [A discharged creditor is still trying to collect. What do I do?](/library/discharged-creditor-still-collecting) for the next steps.

## Not every post-discharge notice is unlawful

A statement concerning a surviving mortgage lien, required information, or a debt outside the discharge is not automatically prohibited collection.

Conversely, a disclaimer saying “this is not an attempt to collect” does not necessarily make a coercive demand lawful. The communication’s substance and circumstances matter.

A bankruptcy court may enforce the discharge and award appropriate relief. Contempt and monetary remedies are not automatic: the Supreme Court’s standard asks whether there was no fair ground of doubt that the discharge barred the conduct.

## The bottom line

A discharged debt does not regain enforceability merely because time passes, the account is sold, or a collector contacts you.

The important questions are whether the debt was covered, whether personal liability was preserved by an enforceable agreement, and whether a lien survived. Revocation requires a court process and specific legal grounds.

Keep your bankruptcy records. If collection resumes, use them to establish what was discharged and address any improper demand promptly.
