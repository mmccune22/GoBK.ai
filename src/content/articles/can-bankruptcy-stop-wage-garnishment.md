## How the stay works

Filing a bankruptcy petition generally triggers the [automatic stay](/library/what-is-the-automatic-stay), which prohibits continuing most garnishments for prefiling debts. You ordinarily do not need a separate hearing to obtain that protection.

Hiring an attorney or preparing to file does not start the stay. The petition must actually be filed, and [prior cases](/library/how-often-can-you-file) or court orders can affect whether protection is available.

Prompt notice helps translate legal protection into a corrected paycheck. The creditor’s attorney, payroll department, and any court or officer administering the garnishment may need the case number, filing date, and supporting notice.

Payroll processing can create delays, but there is no universal rule that a creditor gets another pay period to collect. If another deduction occurs, document it promptly so its treatment can be evaluated.

## Which deductions require different treatment?

### Child support and alimony

Qualifying income withholding for [domestic support obligations](/library/child-support-alimony-and-bankruptcy) can continue during bankruptcy. Those obligations are not discharged.

Chapter 13 may help address arrears, but it does not automatically cancel an existing withholding order. Current support and payments through the plan need to be coordinated.

### Criminal fines and restitution

[Criminal proceedings](/library/court-fines-tickets-restitution) generally are not stayed, and certain criminal collection mechanisms have additional statutory protection. But the fact that a debt is nondischargeable does not, by itself, answer whether every collection method is permitted during bankruptcy.

The particular enforcement order and governing law must be reviewed rather than assuming every court-related wage deduction either stops or continues.

### Taxes and student loans

Wage collection for prefiling [taxes](/library/can-old-tax-debt-be-discharged) and [student loans](/library/student-loans-and-bankruptcy) generally stops when the automatic stay applies, even if the underlying debt will survive discharge.

Stopping collection temporarily and eliminating the debt permanently are separate questions.

### Other payroll deductions

A Chapter 13 plan-payment deduction, voluntary payment authorization, or qualifying [retirement-plan loan](/library/retirement-accounts-in-bankruptcy) deduction is not necessarily treated like an ordinary creditor’s garnishment.

Identify exactly what is being withheld before assuming bankruptcy requires payroll to stop it.

## What happens to money already withheld?

The answer depends on more than the date printed on your paycheck.

Relevant facts can include when wages were earned, when the creditor’s rights attached under state law, when the deduction occurred, and whether the money remains with payroll, a court, or the creditor.

Money withheld after filing may need to be released or returned if collection violated the stay. But neither late notice nor a postfiling deduction automatically resolves ownership or recovery.

### Recovery of prefiling garnishments

Some garnishments shortly before filing may be recoverable as preferential transfers. The usual review period is 90 days, but the trustee must establish the statutory requirements, and defenses or limits may apply.

The often-mentioned $600 figure comes from a specific preference exception in predominantly consumer-debt cases. It is not a promise that any garnishment exceeding that amount will be refunded.

Recovered money may belong to the estate for distribution to creditors. In some circumstances, exemptions and the debtor’s own recovery rights may allow the filer to recover and retain funds. That requires separate analysis and, potentially, legal proceedings.

Keep pay stubs and garnishment records, including amounts taken before and after filing.

## Can the garnishment start again?

### After Chapter 7

If the underlying debt is discharged, the creditor generally cannot resume garnishing your wages to collect that discharged personal liability.

If the debt survives, collection may resume when the relevant stay protection ends. That can occur at discharge, dismissal, or another applicable event—not necessarily when the case closes.

A creditor may also obtain relief from the stay while the case is pending.

### During and after Chapter 13

A covered garnishment generally remains stopped while the applicable protection continues and the debt is handled through the case.

Dismissal, relief from the stay, or another terminating event can allow collection to resume. Successfully completing the plan generally discharges the remaining eligible balance, but nondischargeable obligations may remain enforceable afterward.

## Timing matters—but the whole case matters too

Each deduction can make essential expenses harder to cover. If garnishment is underway, identify the next payday and payroll cutoff promptly.

An emergency filing may be possible, but required credit counseling, eligibility, prior cases, and property risks still need attention. A limited initial filing also creates strict deadlines for the remaining documents.

See [How fast can I file if something is days away?](/library/how-fast-can-i-file) for the emergency-filing process.

## Wage protections may help before bankruptcy

Federal law generally limits ordinary garnishment to the lesser of:

- 25% of disposable earnings.
- The amount by which disposable earnings exceed 30 times the federal minimum hourly wage for a weekly pay period, with adjustments for other pay periods.

“Disposable earnings” generally means earnings after legally required deductions—not simply take-home pay after every voluntary deduction.

Different rules apply to support, taxes, and certain other obligations. State law may provide greater protection, and an exemption or hardship procedure may offer relief without bankruptcy.

## The bottom line

Bankruptcy can often stop ordinary wage garnishment and, if the debt is discharged, prevent it from restarting.

The immediate priorities are identifying the debt, confirming stay protection, and getting notice to everyone handling the deduction. Recovery of money already taken is a separate question.

The filing should address both the garnishment and the financial circumstances that made it difficult to manage.
