## Support obligations survive bankruptcy

Bankruptcy does not eliminate qualifying child support or alimony. This includes past-due amounts, called arrears, as well as ongoing obligations. Related interest and certain obligations owed to government agencies can also qualify as support.

The legal term is **domestic support obligation**. Whether a debt qualifies depends on its nature and the statutory requirements—not simply the label in a divorce decree. An obligation may function as support even if the document calls it something else.

A [hardship discharge](/library/what-if-i-cant-finish-chapter-13) does not provide a way around the support exception.

## Which collection actions can continue?

The [automatic stay](/library/what-is-the-automatic-stay) stops many ordinary debt-collection actions, but support has important exceptions.

Bankruptcy generally does not stop proceedings to establish paternity or establish or modify support. Qualifying income withholding, support-related tax-refund intercepts, and certain license restrictions may also continue.

Other collection methods require closer analysis. For example, the exception allowing collection from property outside the bankruptcy estate does not automatically authorize collection from estate property. That distinction can matter because Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 treat postfiling earnings differently.

**Do not assume that filing Chapter 13 will stop a support garnishment, prevent a license suspension, or cancel a contempt hearing.** The specific enforcement action, court orders, and applicable law determine the answer.

## Support receives priority

Qualifying support arrears have a high priority among unsecured claims.

That does not mean payment is guaranteed or that support always receives every dollar before any other expense. Secured claims, estate-administration costs, and the statutory ordering rules can affect distributions. Certain support claims owed or assigned to government agencies also receive different treatment from support owed directly to a family member.

If a Chapter 7 case has no money available for distribution, priority status does not create funds to pay the arrears. The unpaid support obligation survives.

## How Chapter 13 can help with arrears

Chapter 13 generally requires full payment of priority support arrears through the plan, unless the claim holder agrees to different treatment or a specific statutory exception applies. Certain government-assigned support claims have a limited exception permitting different treatment in a qualifying five-year plan.

For many people, spreading arrears over three to five years is more workable than paying them all at once. But the budget must cover both:

- The [plan payment](/library/chapter-13-monthly-payment), including required payments toward past-due support.
- Current support payments as they come due after filing.

The plan does not automatically replace an existing income-withholding order or eliminate enforcement rights. Payments through the trustee and payments collected elsewhere need to be coordinated and credited accurately.

## Staying current during Chapter 13

Support payments that become due after filing must be current before the court can confirm the plan.

Falling behind afterward can also jeopardize the case. Failure to pay a postfiling domestic support obligation is a statutory ground for dismissal or conversion.

To receive a Chapter 13 discharge, a debtor with a support obligation generally must certify that required amounts due by the certification date have been paid. That includes prefiling amounts to the extent the plan provides for them.

If income drops, address the bankruptcy payment and the support order separately. A lower Chapter 13 payment does not itself reduce court-ordered support.

## Bankruptcy does not change the support order

If your circumstances justify a change in ongoing support, relief generally must be sought in the court or administrative forum with authority over that obligation.

Bankruptcy is not a substitute for a support-modification request. Do not assume a later modification will erase amounts that already accrued; applicable family law controls that question.

Keep records of payments and communicate promptly about discrepancies. If the claimed arrears are wrong, they should be reviewed rather than accepted or ignored simply because a bankruptcy is pending.

## Divorce debts that are not support

Divorce can create separate obligations, such as property-equalization payments or a promise to pay a joint debt and protect a former spouse from collection.

Qualifying nonsupport divorce obligations generally survive Chapter 7 but may be discharged after completing Chapter 13. A Chapter 13 hardship discharge does not provide the same broader protection.

Discharging an obligation to a former spouse also does not necessarily release that spouse from liability to the original joint creditor.

See [Debts from a divorce: what bankruptcy can and can’t touch](/library/divorce-debts-and-bankruptcy) for the distinction.

## The bottom line

Bankruptcy cannot eliminate qualifying child support or alimony. It may still help by clearing other eligible debts and providing a structured way to repay arrears through Chapter 13.

A workable plan must account for current support, past-due support, and any enforcement that can continue. If the support amount itself needs to change, that requires a separate process in the appropriate forum.
