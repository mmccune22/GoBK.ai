## Course one: credit counseling, before you file

Credit counseling is a briefing that reviews your finances, budget, and alternatives to bankruptcy. It generally takes about an hour and must be completed through an approved credit counseling agency.

**When to take it.** Most individual filers must complete counseling within the 180 days before filing. Complete it early enough to avoid delaying your case, but remember that you may need to repeat it if more than 180 days pass before you file.

Limited exceptions exist, but an urgent financial problem does not automatically excuse the requirement. Missing pre-filing counseling can lead to dismissal of the case.

**What it does—and does not—decide.** The counselor may discuss repayment options or a debt management plan. You are not required to accept a proposed plan, and the counselor does not decide whether you are eligible for bankruptcy. Completing counseling also does not commit you to filing.

**What it costs.** Check the provider’s full price, including any certificate fee, before starting. If you cannot afford the charge, ask about a waiver or reduced fee.

**Which provider to use.** Choose an agency [approved for the district](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/credit-counseling-and-debtor-education-courses) where you will file. A general financial education course or a certificate from an unapproved website does not satisfy the requirement.

After completing counseling, obtain your certificate and make sure it is included in the required court filings.

## Course two: debtor education, after you file

Debtor education, also called a personal financial management course, covers topics such as budgeting, managing money, and using credit. It must be completed after your bankruptcy case is filed. You cannot satisfy both requirements by taking both courses before filing.

This course takes at least two hours. Depending on the provider, it may include quizzes or questions to check understanding. Its purpose is education, not deciding whether you deserve debt relief.

**When to take it.** Complete it promptly after filing rather than waiting until the deadline approaches.

The ordinary deadlines for documenting completion are:

- **Chapter 7:** within 60 days after the first date set for the [341 meeting](/library/what-is-a-341-meeting) of creditors. A postponed meeting does not automatically move this deadline.
- **Chapter 13:** no later than the date of the last payment under the plan, or the date a motion for a hardship discharge is filed, if earlier.

The court can extend the filing deadline, but do not assume an extension has been granted.

**Make sure the court receives proof.** Some approved providers notify the court directly. Others issue a certificate that you or your attorney must file. Completing the course does not necessarily mean the court has received the required documentation. Keep your certificate and confirm that completion appears in your case record.

**Why it matters.** Unless an exception applies, you cannot receive a discharge without completing debtor education. A case can close without a discharge if the requirement is not satisfied. Correcting that later may require reopening the case, additional paperwork, and fees.

**What it costs.** Fees vary by provider. Free or reduced-cost services are available based on ability to pay, so ask before paying if cost is a barrier.

If you and your spouse file together, each of you must satisfy both requirements. You may be able to complete sessions together, but each person needs a separate certificate for each requirement.

## The bottom line

Think of these as two separate items on your bankruptcy checklist: credit counseling before filing and debtor education afterward. Plan on roughly three hours total, although the time needed can vary.

Use approved providers, complete the requirements on time, keep your certificates, and make sure the court receives the required proof. Taking care of these steps early helps prevent avoidable delays or a case closing without the discharge you expected.
