## Where to look

- **The NACBA directory.** The National Association of Consumer Bankruptcy Attorneys is an organization of lawyers who represent individuals in bankruptcy. Its public directory lets you search for members by location. Membership is useful information, but it is not a certification or an endorsement of a particular attorney.
- **Your state bar's lawyer referral service.** Many state and local bar associations can connect you with attorneys who handle bankruptcy.
- **Legal aid and pro bono programs.** If cost is the barrier, legal aid offices and volunteer-lawyer programs may help eligible people. Income limits apply, and not every office handles bankruptcy, so ask.
- **A lawyer you already trust.** Attorneys in other fields often know who handles consumer bankruptcy locally.

Look for someone who regularly handles consumer bankruptcy in the district where you would file. Court and trustee practices vary from place to place, and local experience matters.

## What a consultation can help you understand

A consultation usually takes place by phone or video, and sometimes in person, depending on the attorney. It can help you understand:

- Whether bankruptcy fits your situation, and which chapter might apply.
- What could happen to your home, car, accounts, and other property.
- Whether anything is urgent.
- Alternatives to filing, including whether waiting or not filing makes more sense.

Talking to an attorney does not commit you to filing. See [How to prepare for your consultation](/library/how-to-prepare-for-your-consultation) for what to have on hand. Rough information is fine.

## Questions to ask

- Who will handle my case day to day, and who do I contact with questions?
- How do you communicate with clients, and how quickly do you respond?
- What is the fee, and what does it include?
- What could cost extra?
- When is the fee due, and what payment arrangements are available?

[How much does bankruptcy cost?](/library/how-much-does-bankruptcy-cost) explains filing fees, course fees, and how attorney fees are commonly structured in each chapter.

## Consultation charges

Many bankruptcy attorneys offer a free first consultation. Others charge for it. Ask when you schedule.

## Filing without a lawyer

Individuals can legally file bankruptcy without an attorney. Courts publish information for people who represent themselves, and some simple Chapter 7 cases are handled that way.

Self-representation carries more risk when a case involves a home with equity, income near or above the state median, a business, recent transfers or repayments to family, tax debt, or a Chapter 13 plan. Problems in those areas can be costly and are not always obvious from the forms.

Bankruptcy petition preparers can type forms for a fee, but federal law prohibits them from giving legal advice.

Individual advice from an attorney can help identify risks you might not know to look for, even if you ultimately decide to file on your own.
