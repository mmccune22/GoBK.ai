You don't need to have everything figured out first. A consultation usually happens by phone or video, and sometimes in person, depending on the attorney. Bring what you have, approximate where you need to, and ask the attorney what else is needed.

## What the attorney will ask about

These are the subjects bankruptcy law looks at. You don't need to know their legal significance. Rough answers are fine, and "I'm not sure" is a fine answer too.

- **Debts and collection activity.** Who you owe, roughly how much, and what kind: credit cards, medical bills, taxes, student loans, support, car or home loans. Mention any lawsuit, judgment, garnishment, or repossession. Your [free credit reports](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/) can help you remember debts, though they won't show everything.
- **Household income and expenses.** Income from every source for the past six months, for everyone in the household, and your regular monthly expenses.
- **Property, accounts, and loan balances.** Your home and vehicles, roughly what they're worth and what's owed; bank and payment-app accounts; retirement accounts; anything you own with someone else.
- **Recent payments, transfers, prior bankruptcies, and expected money.** Payments to family or friends in the past year or so; anything sold, given away, or retitled; large purchases or cash advances; any prior bankruptcy; and money you expect, such as a tax refund, inheritance, settlement, or claim.
- **Urgent deadlines.** A foreclosure sale date, a repossession, a garnishment, or a court date. Mention these first.

Also mention a business you own or recently closed, a divorce or support order, anyone who cosigned for you, and any unfiled tax returns.

## Helpful documents

**For a first consultation**, whatever is easy to find helps: a recent pay stub or benefit letter, your most recent tax return, recent statements for your main debts and loans, and any lawsuit or garnishment papers.

**Before filing**, your attorney will tell you exactly what is needed. Requirements vary by court and case, but commonly include:

- Pay stubs or other income records for the period before filing.
- Your most recent federal tax return.
- Bank and account statements.
- Statements for debts, vehicles, your home, and retirement accounts.
- Photo identification and proof of your Social Security number for the meeting of creditors.

Missing records are common. Tax transcripts, reprinted statements, and credit reports can often fill the gaps.

Before sending sensitive documents to anyone, confirm who you're working with and how your information will be handled.

## Questions to ask

- What are my options, including not filing?
- What would happen to my property?
- What will this cost?
- Is anything urgent?
- What should I do, or avoid doing, right now?
