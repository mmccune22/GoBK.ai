## A personal relationship does not remove the debt

If your parents lent you $5,000 with an understanding that you would repay it, that obligation generally belongs in your bankruptcy paperwork alongside your other debts. An informal or undocumented loan still needs to be disclosed.

A gift is different from a loan. Describe what actually happened rather than retroactively labeling family help as debt—or calling a genuine loan a gift to avoid listing it.

The family member or friend generally will receive creditor notices. You cannot keep a debt outside the case simply because you want to preserve the relationship.

An ordinary unsecured family loan is generally eligible for discharge. Chapter 7 may eliminate personal liability, while Chapter 13 generally treats the claim under the repayment plan. A valid lien or a discharge exception can change the result.

## Why repayments before filing receive scrutiny

It is understandable to want to repay someone who helped you before dealing with banks and other creditors. But bankruptcy law limits certain payments that favor one creditor over others.

A [trustee](/library/who-is-the-trustee) may seek to recover a qualifying **preferential payment** made within 90 days before filing. If the benefited creditor was an insider, the review period can extend to one year.

Relatives commonly qualify as insiders. A friend is not automatically an insider merely because you are close, although the relationship and circumstances may support that classification.

Recovery requires more than showing that money changed hands. The trustee must establish the statutory requirements, including insolvency at the relevant time and that the payment gave the creditor more than they would have received in the specified Chapter 7 comparison. Defenses and statutory limits may apply.

Repaying a real debt can therefore create a problem even when no one intended to do anything dishonest.

## If you already repaid someone

Disclose the payment accurately. Include the amount, date, recipient, reason, and source of the money, and keep supporting records.

Do not assume a small payment will be ignored or a large payment will automatically be recovered. The facts, available defenses, and benefit to the estate matter.

If the trustee seeks repayment, the recipient may receive a demand or lawsuit. That does not necessarily mean the relative or friend committed fraud; preference recovery is different from a finding of wrongdoing.

Do not try to undo the transaction informally, backdate documents, or move money again to conceal what happened. Further transfers can create additional issues.

## Property transfers and co-signed loans need attention too

Paying a relative does not have to involve cash. Transferring a vehicle or another asset to satisfy an old loan can also be reviewed.

Giving away property, selling it below value, or granting a new lien to secure an old family debt can raise separate transfer issues. Calling the transaction a repayment does not automatically protect it.

Also disclose loans where a family member co-signed or guaranteed your debt. Paying the bank may benefit the family guarantor, and your discharge generally does not eliminate [that person’s separate liability](/library/what-cant-bankruptcy-fix).

The analysis looks at who benefited, not just whose name appeared on the payment.

## Can I voluntarily repay them later?

Yes. The Bankruptcy Code expressly allows voluntary repayment of a discharged debt.

That does not restore the creditor’s right to compel payment or permit collection pressure that [violates the discharge](/library/can-a-discharged-debt-come-back). You may choose to help when your finances allow, without sacrificing the legal protection you received.

The timing and source of funds still matter. Do not assume that receiving a discharge means every asset has been released from the bankruptcy estate. In [Chapter 13](/library/how-chapter-13-works), income and payments remain subject to the plan and applicable court requirements while the case is pending.

Voluntary repayment should use funds you are legally free to spend, without interfering with required payments or estate administration.

## The bottom line

Family and friendship do not make a genuine loan exempt from disclosure or automatically protect prefiling repayments from review.

List the debts, disclose prior payments and transfers, and identify any collateral or guarantees. Do not rearrange assets to protect someone from the bankruptcy process.

If an eligible loan is discharged, voluntary repayment can remain an option when your finances and legal obligations allow it. Bankruptcy can relieve the enforceable debt without deciding what generosity your future budget may support.
