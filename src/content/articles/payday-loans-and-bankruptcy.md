## Payday loans are generally dischargeable

An ordinary unsecured payday loan generally receives the same bankruptcy treatment as other unsecured personal loans.

In Chapter 7, an eligible balance can be discharged without requiring repayment from future income. In Chapter 13, the lender generally receives the treatment required for unsecured creditors under the plan, with any remaining eligible balance discharged after successful completion.

Repeated renewals, high interest, or accumulated fees do not, by themselves, make the debt nondischargeable. The lender’s claim still must be legally enforceable, and ordinary discharge exceptions can apply.

List the loan even if you dispute its validity, believe the lender is unlicensed, or no longer know who owns the account.

## Checks and automatic withdrawals need attention

Payday lenders often hold a postdated check or authorization to withdraw money electronically. Bankruptcy does not necessarily prevent every payment attempt from reaching your account.

The [automatic stay](/library/what-is-the-automatic-stay) has a specific exception for presenting a negotiable instrument, which can include a check. Presenting the check, receiving payment, and retaining the proceeds can raise different legal questions. A withdrawal after filing is therefore not automatically a stay violation or automatically recoverable.

Electronic payment authorizations require separate attention. You generally can revoke an authorization for recurring electronic withdrawals and notify your bank, but the timing and procedures matter. A stop-payment order may also be appropriate.

Keep copies of notices and confirmations. **Stopping an automatic payment does not cancel the loan.** Tell your attorney about outstanding checks and payment authorizations before filing rather than assuming bankruptcy will block them.

## What about a lender’s fraud claim?

Taking out a loan shortly before filing does not automatically establish fraud. Being unable to repay is not, by itself, enough.

But a creditor may challenge discharge if the debt was obtained through fraud or qualifying false representations. Certain [recent luxury purchases and qualifying cash advances](/library/should-i-stop-paying-my-credit-cards) also have special statutory presumptions.

Not every payday loan falls within the cash-advance presumption, and borrowing for necessities does not automatically resolve every fraud question. The transaction and surrounding facts matter.

Do not take a new loan intending to discharge it instead of repaying it. If you recently borrowed, disclose when, why, and what representations you made.

## Title loans involve rights in your car

A typical title loan uses your vehicle as collateral. Bankruptcy may discharge eligible personal liability while leaving a valid lien intact.

That means eliminating the debt as a personal obligation does not necessarily let you keep the car without paying or otherwise addressing the lender’s secured claim.

Some arrangements are legally structured as title pawns rather than ordinary secured loans. Under certain state laws, ownership rights can expire if the vehicle is not redeemed by a deadline. Filing bankruptcy does not necessarily turn an expired or limited ownership right into full ownership.

The agreement, state law, and status of any redemption period must be reviewed before assuming the loan can be restructured.

## Options for a title loan in Chapter 7

Depending on the transaction and your ownership rights, options may include:

- **Redemption.** For a qualifying vehicle securing a dischargeable consumer debt, paying the allowed secured claim in a lump sum may release the lien. Statutory eligibility and valuation rules apply.
- **A retention arrangement.** Continuing payments, negotiating terms, or reaffirming may be available, but keeping the car is not guaranteed merely because payments continue.
- **Surrender.** Giving up the vehicle may allow eligible remaining personal liability to be discharged. Surrender itself does not create a discharge.

Reaffirmation preserves personal liability that otherwise could be eliminated, so its consequences deserve careful attention. See [Keeping a car in Chapter 7](/library/reaffirmation-retain-redeem).

## Options for a title loan in Chapter 13

If you retain the necessary ownership rights, Chapter 13 may let you restructure a title lender’s secured claim.

A qualifying plan may reduce the secured portion to the vehicle’s value, treat the remaining balance as unsecured, and establish a court-approved interest rate and repayment schedule.

The [910-day restriction](/library/chapter-13-keep-car-cramdown) for certain vehicle-purchase loans often does not apply to a loan secured by a car you already owned. Other requirements still matter, and neither a balance reduction nor a particular interest rate is guaranteed.

You must fund the plan and maintain required insurance. Title-pawn deadlines and state-law ownership issues can limit these options.

## If repossession is approaching

Filing generally stops a pending repossession when the automatic stay applies and the vehicle is protected by it. Prior cases and other exceptions can affect that protection.

If the car has already been taken, filing does not automatically require its immediate return. Your remaining ownership rights, any sale, and the required court procedures matter.

See [Can bankruptcy stop a repossession, or get my car back?](/library/stop-repossession-get-car-back) in “Urgent problems.”

## The bottom line

Payday and title loans often arise when a household is struggling to cover basic expenses. There is no reason to treat them as a source of shame.

Bankruptcy can generally address eligible unsecured payday debt. Title loans require an additional decision about the vehicle, the lender’s rights, and whether keeping it is legally possible and affordable.

Address payment authorizations and repossession deadlines promptly, and evaluate these loans as part of the household’s overall debt and budget.
