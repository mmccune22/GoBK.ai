## Where AI can help

- **Understanding terms.** Asking an AI tool to explain the automatic stay, a reaffirmation agreement, or the means test, and following up until it makes sense, can be a good use of it.
- **Organizing your facts.** AI can help you turn notes into a list of debts, a summary of income, or a timeline of what happened and when.
- **Preparing questions.** Asking what someone in your situation might want to ask a bankruptcy attorney can produce a useful starting list.

## Where it can go wrong

- **It only knows what you tell it.** Important details, such as a repayment to a relative, a clause in a divorce decree, or an unfiled tax year, are easy to leave out if you don't know they matter.
- **Rules vary and change.** Exemptions, income figures, and court practices differ by state and district, and dollar amounts are updated periodically. AI tools may rely on outdated or general information.
- **It can be confidently wrong.** AI tools sometimes state incorrect rules or cite sources that don't exist.
- **It isn't responsible for the result.** A conclusion about your case still needs to be checked against your actual facts and current law.

## Bringing AI questions to an attorney

It's fine to mention that you've used AI, and to bring questions it helped you think of. Share your facts and questions, and treat any AI conclusions as a starting point for the conversation rather than the answer.

If an AI tool suggests moving money, transferring property, repaying someone, or withdrawing retirement funds, get advice before acting. [What should I avoid before filing bankruptcy?](/library/what-to-avoid-before-filing) explains why.

## Protecting your privacy

- Don't share Social Security numbers, full account numbers, passwords, or login credentials.
- Describe your situation in general terms and rough numbers when that's enough.
- Before uploading documents, review the service's privacy and security practices, including how long it keeps information and how it may be used.

A conversation with a general-purpose AI tool is not the same as a confidential conversation with your attorney.
