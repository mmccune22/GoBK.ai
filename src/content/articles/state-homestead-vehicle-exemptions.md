## How to read this table

- **These are equity figures, not value figures.** Equity is generally the property’s value minus the loans secured by it. A $400,000 house with a $340,000 mortgage has about $60,000 of equity, and that is the amount a homestead exemption is measured against.
- **The table summarizes the rules; it cannot capture every condition.** Exemptions can depend on ownership, residence, how property is used, age, disability, required declarations, and other requirements that do not fit in a table cell.
- **Amounts change.** Several states adjust their figures automatically on a set schedule, and legislatures amend them. This article was last updated on November 2, 2026. In November 2026, we rechecked the figures against state statutes and official sources where we could reach them. Entries marked ‡ could not be confirmed against a current authoritative source and should be treated as approximate. Some states have changes scheduled or pending, which the table notes where we know of them.
- **Some states offer a choice.** Some states let eligible filers choose the federal bankruptcy exemptions instead of the state’s system. For cases filed on or after April 1, 2025, the federal list protects up to $31,575 of home equity and up to $5,025 of vehicle equity, along with other categories. You generally choose one system rather than combining the best parts of each, and which system works better depends on what you own.
- **Married filers.** Whether exemption amounts apply per person, per household, or per property depends on the state and on which system is used. Where a state sets a separate joint figure, the table notes it.
- **No vehicle-specific exemption.** In states without one, a wildcard or general personal-property exemption may protect some vehicle equity. Whether it does, and how much, depends on the state’s rules and your other property.
- **Recent moves matter.** If you have not lived in your current state for about two years, federal rules may require using another state’s exemptions. See [What are exemptions?](/library/what-are-bankruptcy-exemptions)

## Important: confirm the rules before relying on these figures

This table provides general educational information, not legal advice or a determination that your property is protected. Exemption amounts, eligibility requirements, and court interpretations can change. Although we work to keep this guide accurate, we do not guarantee that every figure is current, complete, or applicable to your circumstances.

Before relying on any amount shown here, confirm the current law and its application with your own independent bankruptcy attorney licensed in the relevant jurisdiction. This applies to every entry, including figures not marked as approximate or disputed.

Your current state of residence does not necessarily determine which exemptions apply. Recent moves, ownership arrangements, joint filing, age, disability, required declarations, and federal limits can affect the protection available. An exemption also generally does not eliminate a mortgage, car loan, or other valid lien.

Do not use this table alone to decide whether to file bankruptcy, transfer or sell property, withdraw retirement funds, or stop making secured-debt payments. Reading this guide does not create an attorney-client relationship with GoBK or any attorney identified on this site.

## What to do with your number

Use the table to identify questions about your property—not to make a final decision about whether it is safe.

Start by estimating your equity: the property’s current value minus the balances of loans secured by it. Then confirm which exemption system applies, the current exemption amount, and any conditions or limits.

Equity below a listed amount does not, by itself, guarantee that you can keep the property. Equity above it does not automatically mean you will lose it. Liens, ownership interests, sale costs, other available exemptions, and the bankruptcy chapter can affect the outcome.

Before acting, have your own independent bankruptcy attorney verify the figures and evaluate your circumstances under the law applicable when you file.

## The bottom line

This table is a starting point for understanding homestead and vehicle exemptions. It is not a guarantee that a particular amount applies to you or that your property is protected. Confirm the current figures, eligibility rules, and treatment of your property with your own independent bankruptcy attorney before making decisions.
