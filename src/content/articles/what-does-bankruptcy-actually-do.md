Strip away the fear and the jargon, and bankruptcy is two legal mechanisms with a lot of paperwork around them.

## Mechanism one: the stop

The moment a case is filed, a federal injunction called the automatic stay halts essentially all collection: garnishments, lawsuits, foreclosures, repossessions, levies, calls. No judge signs anything; the filing itself is the order. This is the emergency brake, and it's why bankruptcy can help on a Friday when the sale is Monday. It has its own full explanation in the Urgent problems section.

## Mechanism two: the ending

At the end of a successful case comes the discharge: a court order permanently erasing your legal responsibility for the covered debts. Not a pause, not a settlement, an ending, backed by its own injunction against anyone ever trying to collect again. Most unsecured debt (cards, medical, personal loans) discharges; a defined list (support, recent taxes, most student loans, fines) survives. The Debts section maps that line in detail.

## The machinery between

Everything else you've heard of serves those two mechanisms. The **chapters** are just two routes to the discharge: Chapter 7 in about four months for those who qualify, Chapter 13 through a three-to-five-year plan that also fixes problems 7 can't, like a defaulted mortgage. The **trustee** audits the paperwork. **Exemptions** decide what you keep, and they're generous enough that the typical case is "everything." The **341 meeting** is five minutes of questions. The whole thing happens in federal court and almost never in a courtroom.

## What it is not

Not jail-adjacent: there are no debtors' prisons, and honest filers face no punishment beyond the process itself. Not a moral verdict: the Constitution itself told Congress to create bankruptcy laws, and the people who use them are overwhelmingly ordinary households hit by job loss, illness, and divorce. Not rare, and not permanent ruin: credit for most filers begins recovering within a couple of years, covered honestly in the Life-after section.

## The bottom line

Stop, then end. If those are the two things your situation needs, bankruptcy may be its shape; the Checkup and the rest of this Library are for figuring out whether it is, and which route, or whether something short of it does the job.
