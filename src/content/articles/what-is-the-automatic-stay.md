## What it is

The automatic stay is a legal protection that generally begins when a bankruptcy petition is filed. In an ordinary voluntary case, you do not need a separate hearing or injunction before it takes effect.

It generally restricts collection of prefiling debts and protects property covered by the bankruptcy estate. Hiring an attorney, completing credit counseling, or preparing paperwork does not start that protection.

Creditors do not need to agree to the stay. Prompt notice still matters: a payroll department, collection attorney, or repossession company may need the case information before it can stop an action already underway.

Lack of notice does not necessarily make an otherwise prohibited action valid, but whether it must be reversed—and whether damages are available—requires separate analysis.

## What it generally stops

When the stay applies, it can stop:

- [Garnishment](/library/can-bankruptcy-stop-wage-garnishment) for ordinary unsecured debts.
- Pending [foreclosure](/library/can-bankruptcy-stop-foreclosure) sales and [repossessions](/library/stop-repossession-get-car-back).
- [Lawsuits](/library/served-with-a-lawsuit) seeking to collect prefiling debts.
- Enforcement of many prefiling judgments.
- Many [bank levies](/library/bank-account-frozen-levy) and tax-collection actions.
- Calls, letters, and other demands seeking payment of covered debts.

The protection is broad, but it is not a universal freeze on every legal proceeding or financial transaction.

Filing before a sale or seizure can be very different from filing afterward. The stay generally prevents prohibited new action; it does not automatically reverse completed transactions, release every bank hold, or require immediate return of a repossessed car. Additional legal steps may be necessary.

## Important exceptions

### Support and family matters

Proceedings to establish or modify [support](/library/child-support-alimony-and-bankruptcy), determine paternity, address custody, or dissolve a marriage generally can continue. Dividing property belonging to the bankruptcy estate presents a separate issue.

Qualifying support withholding, tax-refund intercepts, and certain license restrictions may also continue.

### Criminal proceedings

Bankruptcy generally does not stop [criminal proceedings](/library/court-fines-tickets-restitution), including a prosecution involving a bad check. Filing does not cancel criminal-court obligations.

### Government and tax activity

Certain police and regulatory actions can proceed. Tax authorities may conduct audits, assess taxes, and issue specified notices even when collection is restricted.

Some tax-refund offsets also have statutory exceptions.

### Evictions

A prefiling judgment for possession can allow an eviction to proceed, subject to limited exceptions and certification procedures. Other exceptions involve alleged property endangerment or illegal drug use.

A tenant should not assume filing automatically prevents removal.

### Checks and other transactions

Presenting a negotiable instrument, such as a qualifying check, has a specific stay exception. That does not necessarily resolve whether resulting funds may lawfully be retained.

## Utilities have separate protections

Bankruptcy law generally prohibits a covered utility from refusing or discontinuing service solely because you filed or failed to pay a prefiling utility bill.

But continued service may require a deposit or other adequate assurance of future payment. In Chapter 7 and Chapter 13, the relevant statutory period generally is 20 days after filing.

You must also address charges for new service. Bankruptcy does not provide indefinite utility service without payment, and not every service provider qualifies as a utility under this rule.

## Does it protect a co-signer?

Your automatic stay generally does not protect another person simply because they share responsibility for your debt.

Chapter 13 provides a separate [co-debtor stay](/library/what-cant-bankruptcy-fix) for qualifying consumer debts. It can temporarily restrict collection from an individual co-borrower or someone who pledged collateral for the debt.

Exceptions apply, and the creditor can seek relief—for example, to the extent the plan does not propose to pay the claim. This protection does not discharge the other person’s liability.

## Repeat filings can limit protection

[Prior cases](/library/how-often-can-you-file) must be reviewed before relying on a new filing.

If one prior case was pending and dismissed during the preceding year, a statutory rule can terminate stay protection after 30 days unless the court extends it. Courts differ on the scope of that termination.

With two or more qualifying prior dismissed cases during that year, the stay generally does not arise unless the court imposes it.

Exceptions apply. Requests to extend or impose protection have strict timing and good-faith requirements. In particular, a request to extend the stay generally requires a hearing completed before the 30-day period expires.

Certain prior orders affecting real estate can also restrict protection in a later case. A new case number alone does not guarantee that a scheduled foreclosure or other collection action must stop.

## How long does the stay last?

Different parts of the stay can end at different times.

Dismissal, discharge, case closure, changes in estate-property status, or a court order can end particular protections. A secured creditor may ask for relief from the stay to pursue collateral, such as when required payments or insurance are not maintained.

After discharge, a separate [discharge injunction](/library/can-a-discharged-debt-come-back) generally prohibits collection of covered debts as personal liabilities. Surviving liens and nondischargeable obligations require separate attention.

## If a creditor keeps collecting

Keep letters, voicemails, payment records, garnishment notices, and screenshots. Record when the creditor received notice of the filing, if known, and tell your attorney promptly.

For an individual injured by a willful stay violation, the Code provides actual damages, including costs and attorney fees, and allows punitive damages in appropriate circumstances. Relief depends on the applicable requirements and evidence; a contact or withdrawal does not automatically establish a compensable violation.

Do not assume an ongoing action is lawful—or that it guarantees a damages award. Identify what happened, whether the stay covered it, and what correction or court relief is needed.

## The bottom line

The automatic stay can provide immediate, powerful protection from many collection actions. Its practical value is the opportunity to address the underlying financial problem before collection continues.

Timing, prior cases, exceptions, and court orders matter. Stopping a foreclosure or repossession is the beginning of the analysis; keeping the property requires a workable solution for the debt afterward.
